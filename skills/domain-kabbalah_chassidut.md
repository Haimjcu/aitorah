# Skill: Kabbalah & Chassidut

## When this applies
Zohar, Kabbalah (sefirot, divine names, Arizal, Ramak), and chassidut (Baal Shem Tov, Tanya and Chabad, Breslov / Likutei Moharan, Kedushat Levi, Sefat Emet, Mei HaShiloach, Piaseczna Rebbe, Rav Tzadok). Many of these users write in French. Real requests: "the Zohar on today's parasha", "the sod of the Ten Commandments", "learning sefirot before age 15/40", "Likutei Moharan on speech", "Breslov vs. Bunin's outlook".

## Research path
1. **Find the exact source.** Book names on Sefaria vary. Use find_ref when unsure, then get_text. Examples:
   - Zohar by parasha: `Zohar, Bereshit 1:1`, `Zohar, Bo 1:1`
   - `Tanya, Part I; Likkutei Amarim 2`
   - `Likutei Moharan 1:1`
   - `Kedushat Levi, Genesis, Bereshit`
   - `Sefat Emet, Genesis, Bereshit` (year sections)
   - `Mei HaShiloach, Volume I, Genesis, Bereshit`
   - Esh Kodesh and other works not listed here: find_ref first, then a specific section
2. **Parasha-based teachings:** get the parasha from the calendar tool if needed. Then search_texts with category "Chasidut" or "Kabbalah" and the parasha name, or get_links on the parasha's first verse with category "Chasidut" / "Kabbalah".
3. **Concepts** (sefirot, tzimtzum, bittul, devekut): get_topic for the concept, then read 1–3 classic sources.

## How to answer
- Attribute every idea to a specific book and author. "Chassidut teaches…" is too vague; say "The Baal HaTanya explains in chapter 2…".
- Explain mystical concepts in clear language, with an analogy where the source itself uses one. Distinguish what the text says from interpretation.
- Different schools disagree; don't flatten them. Chabad, Breslov, Polish chassidut, and the Lithuanian/Kabbalistic tradition of the Vilna Gaon and Ramchal can see a question differently.
- **Practices** (meditation, kavanot, divine names, segulot):
  - Present what the sources describe, and the traditional cautions: who learned Kabbalah and when, and not pronouncing divine names.
  - Do not give instructions for practices the tradition restricts.
  - Steer toward learning with a teacher.
- **Questions about age or prerequisites for learning Kabbalah:** present the sources and the range of views (Shach, the Ari's students, Chassidic openness to Tanya-level study).

## Pitfalls
- Quoting the Zohar or the Rebbe from memory with invented chapter references. Retrieve the source or cite without numbers.
- New Age or non-Jewish interpretations of Kabbalah presented as Jewish tradition.
- Overclaiming certainty about esoteric meanings.

## Citation format
[Tanya, Part I; Likkutei Amarim 2], [Likutei Moharan 1:1], [Zohar, Bereshit 1:1], [Sefat Emet, Genesis, Bereshit 1]
