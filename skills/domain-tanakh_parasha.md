# Skill: Tanakh & Parasha (Torah, Prophets, Writings, commentators, midrash)

## When this applies
Questions about verses, stories, and figures in Tanakh; the weekly parasha; what a commentator says (Rashi, Ramban, Ibn Ezra, Sforno, Or HaChaim, Kli Yakar, Malbim…); midrash; and requests for divrei Torah or drashot. This is the largest area (~21% of questions). Common requests: "meaning of Genesis 1:1", "Kli Yakar summary on Sukkot", "dvar Torah for parashat Noach", "why is Moses' face shining".

## Research path
1. **Read the verse(s) first** with get_text (`Genesis 1:1-3`). Quote the Hebrew and give a translation.
2. **Commentators:** get_links on the verse with category "Commentary" to see who comments. Then get_text the exact comment (`Rashi on Genesis 1:1:1`, `Ramban on Genesis 1:1:1`, `Kli Yakar on Exodus 20:8:1`). Read what the commentator actually says before summarizing it.
3. **Midrash:** get_links category "Midrash", or search_texts (category "Midrash") for Bereshit Rabbah, Tanchuma, and so on.
4. **Parasha questions:** call the calendar tool for this week's parasha (Israel vs. Diaspora can differ), then read its opening verses.
5. **Thematic questions** ("what does the Torah say about X"): get_topic for the theme, or search_texts. Then read the 2–4 most relevant verses.

## How to answer
- Start with *pshat* (plain meaning), then bring commentators, noting where they disagree and why: the textual difficulty each one is solving.
- Attribute every interpretation to its source. Never blend several commentators into one voice.
- Midrash is not pshat: say when an idea is midrashic.
- **Divrei Torah / drashot:** write a complete piece with a title, an opening question, 2–3 sources developed, and a closing message. Usual length is 500–900 words unless asked otherwise. Base it on real sources you retrieved. Mark your own creative connections as yours.
- Long requests (summaries of a commentator across a parasha): cover it completely. If the answer will be long, organize by verse with headers.

## Pitfalls seen in real chats
- Attributing an idea to Rashi or Ramban that they don't say (the most common pushback in this area). Read the comment.
- Using Christian translations or verse numbering (KJV) without saying so. Use Jewish translations (Sefaria's JPS/Koren). Where chapter or verse numbering differs, note it.
- Treating the Kli Yakar, Or HaChaim, or other later commentators as if they were pshat commentators.
- Paraphrasing a commentator in quotation marks.

## Citation format
[Genesis 1:1], [Rashi on Genesis 1:1:1], [Ramban on Exodus 20:8:1], [Bereshit Rabbah 1:1], [Kli Yakar on Leviticus 23:40:1]
